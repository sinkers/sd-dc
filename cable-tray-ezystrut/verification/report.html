Key Difference: External vs Internal

External (UP): Arc centre above. Cable on outside (tension). No hold-downs needed on bend. Gravity assists.

Internal (DOWN): Arc centre below. Cable on inside (compression). Hold-down clips MANDATORY. Gravity works against cable retention.

Data Centre Application

Overhead tray → drop to switchboard entry.
Elevated cable corridor → under-floor distribution.
Mezzanine level → ground floor equipment.
Drop to rack-mounted PDU from overhead tray.

4. Connection Rules Applied

1
Each rail is a DISCRETE piece. Rails never merge or pass through joints.
2
Splice plates sit on OUTSIDE of rails, bridging the gap between two rail ends. Smooth bolt heads face inward (won't snag cables).
3
Elbows are SEPARATE fittings with own inner + outer curved rails. NOT a deformation of the straight rail.
4
At a tee, the rail on the branch side is CUT AWAY. Cables must be able to physically pass from main to branch.
5
TX brackets reinforce structural gaps where rail material was removed (tees and crosses).
6
Rungs are absent in junction openings. No rung should block a cable transition path.
7
Each elbow connection = 2 rail-to-rail joints (inner + outer). Each joint = 1 splice plate. Total per elbow = 4 splice plates.
